Canada's top court largely upholds Quebec ban on religious symbols for public servants

Closely watched decision exposes tensions between minority rights and the power of government to override those rights Canada’s top court has largely upheld a controversial secularism law in the province of Quebec barring public sector workers from wearing conspicuous religious symbols, in a closely watched decision that exposes the tensions between minority rights and the power of government to override those rights. In a 6-1 ruling released on Friday morning , the supreme court found Quebec was permitted to shield the law, Bill 21, from most court challenges on the grounds that it violated charter rights.
